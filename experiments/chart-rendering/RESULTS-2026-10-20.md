# Chart.js versus the production SVG chart

Measured October 20, 2026, 19:12–19:19 America/Los_Angeles, on Apple M3,
macOS 27.0 (26A428), headed Chrome 156.0.8078.47, Node 24.15.0.
Chart.js was pinned to 4.5.1. Production chart source was unchanged from `c803a59`.
The benchmark began at `dbf0645`; after a rejected trial, the remaining four
trials used `7e65a43`, which adds process-tree retry/resume handling without
changing the chart adapter or workload.

## Result

**Chart.js is a plausible route to smoother rendering, but this experiment does
not establish a reliable reduction in total browser CPU.** At matched paint cadence,
its average CPU was effectively the same as the current SVG chart. At browser
refresh rate it painted approximately twice as often with lower average CPU, but
the trial-to-trial range was too large to treat the average as a dependable gain.

The production renderer remains unchanged. The adapter is retained as a benchmark
prototype, not shipped to dashboard users.

| Renderer | Average CPU, one-core % | CPU range across two trials | Actual paints/s | Average renderer task CPU % | Post-GC JS heap MiB |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Current SVG, 20 Hz ceiling | 16.0 | 14.1–17.9 | 15.7 | 5.32 | 1.46 |
| Chart.js, 20 Hz paint ceiling | 16.3 | 14.5–18.0 | 15.0 | 4.34 | 2.94 |
| Chart.js, native animation | 13.6 | 8.7–18.5 | 30.0 | 3.86 | 2.93 |
| SVG, paint ceiling removed | 16.8 | 15.7–18.0 | 30.9 | 6.00 | 1.53 |

Chart.js native animation averaged 15% less total browser CPU than current SVG,
and 19% less than uncapped SVG. These are descriptive averages, not a validated
power/performance improvement: the forward comparison favored Chart.js, while
the reverse comparison favored current SVG. Capped Chart.js did not reproduce
a whole-browser CPU advantage. Renderer task CPU was lower on average with
Chart.js, but excludes other browser processes and cannot replace the total.

Chart.js animation painted with a median gap near 33.3 ms and p95 near 34.5 ms;
current SVG's corresponding gaps were about 66.7 ms and 68.1 ms. The native
refresh cadence observed in this run was approximately 30 Hz, so the `svg60`
variant name does not mean it achieved 60 Hz. Likewise a 20 Hz ceiling produced
roughly 15–16 actual paints/s. These are draw callbacks/DOM mutation timings,
not proof of GPU presentation timing or perceptual smoothness on every display.

Whole-browser RSS phase medians ranged from 863 to 1,099 MiB across trials,
including shared pages and Chrome infrastructure. There was no persuasive
memory-footprint advantage. Chart.js added about 1.5 MiB to the page's post-GC
JavaScript heap. The locally served UMD bundle is 208,522 bytes, or 70,736 bytes
with gzip; this dependency is currently confined to the experiment.

## Method and individual observations

The [benchmark instructions](README.md) describe the exact workload and commands.
Each trial used a fresh isolated browser, five seconds of warmup with an already
full 60-point history, then approximately 30 seconds of measurement. The complete
chart was visible at 1280 × 1100, device scale 1. All variants used the same
deterministic history, dashboard updates every 100 ms, history updates every
second, and 1,000 ms linear animation. Chart.js used monotone cubic curves, dots,
fill, sorted normalized data, disabled parsing and explicit scale bounds.
It retained point identity as the rolling window advanced.

| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | Current SVG | 30.88 | 17.88 | 15.41 |
| 2 | Chart.js capped | 30.77 | 14.53 | 14.98 |
| 3 | Chart.js native | 30.57 | 8.70 | 30.00 |
| 4 | SVG uncapped | 30.79 | 17.96 | 30.98 |
| 5 | SVG uncapped | 30.85 | 15.69 | 30.76 |
| 6 | Chart.js native | 30.99 | 18.46 | 30.01 |
| 7 | Chart.js capped | 30.96 | 17.99 | 15.02 |
| 8 | Current SVG | 30.92 | 14.10 | 15.98 |

One additional uncapped-SVG trial was rejected because Chrome changed its process
tree during measurement. Its CPU result was not retained or included. CPU sums
require the same process IDs at both boundaries; otherwise exited children can
make a simple cumulative-time delta misleading. All eight accepted trials had
stable process IDs at those boundaries.

All accepted trials passed assertions for plot-edge alignment, 60 finite point
coordinates, scrolling at three intermediate positions throughout the second,
and frozen coordinates after stopping. No uncaught page errors were observed
during the measured phases. Fixed-history screenshots were visually reviewed for
the SVG and Canvas prototypes; grid, labels, curve shape, dots and fill remain
comparable. All trials had one document at the heap observation.

This isolates renderer behavior rather than exercising the complete ingestion
and SSE path. It does not validate production resizing, hover accessibility,
visibility/reconnect behavior, final-state integration or offline Rust asset
bundling for Chart.js. It is not a long-duration leak test. The sample size is
two trials per variant on one machine, with unpinned CPU frequency and normal
desktop activity. A migration should first reproduce a CPU advantage across
longer paired trials and complete those integration checks.

Raw measurements and screenshots are retained locally under
`work/chart-benchmark/`; `work/chart-benchmark-run.txt` includes the discarded
trial's process-tree error. Earlier smoke-test artifacts are under
`work/chart-benchmark-smoke*`.
